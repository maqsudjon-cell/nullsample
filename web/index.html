Why that matters

  • Nothing to license, nobody to sue you. Generators built on sample packs or trained on recordings leave a question about what went in and who owns what comes out. There are no recordings here at all, so the question does not arise.
  • A seed reproduces a track exactly. Same seed, same audio, on any machine, in any browser, today or in five years. So you can send someone a seed instead of a file, and ask for a change instead of starting again.
  • Stems come out already separated — drums, bass, lead, arp, pads, effects — because the engine never merged them in the first place. No source separation, no artefacts, just the parts as they were synthesised.

Who it is for

People who need music they can use without reading a licence first.

  • Indie game developers — a loop for a level, and the stems to duck under dialogue.
  • Video and podcast creators — beds and stings that will not trip a content claim, because there is no recording to match against.
  • Streamers — background music that survives a VOD.
